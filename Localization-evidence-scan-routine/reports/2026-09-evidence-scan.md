## Cross-cutting themes

### Theme 1: Local actors carry the work but hold little of the funding or formal power

**ALNAP** — *State of the Humanitarian System (SOHS) 2026* (full report published 10 September 2026; assesses 2022–2025). Chapter 4 examines "how far localisation and accountability reforms have moved from rhetoric to reality." The in-brief summary reports that local and national actors received "4.3% of humanitarian funding directly" and "8.7% including indirect funding" in 2025. National NGOs held "11% of Humanitarian Country Team seats, versus 79% by UN agencies, INGOs and donors." In Ukraine, international UN personnel earned "17 times more than national L/NNGO staff."

**Peace Direct** — *From Participation to Influence: Reflections from the First UN Peacebuilding Week* (2 September 2026, Ana Escobar, UN Representative). The article argues that local peacebuilders must move from being consulted to shaping decisions, and that meaningful participation requires local actors to help define success metrics, control resources and shape policy. It recommends that UN–Civil Society Dialogue outcomes connect to decision-making bodies (DPPA, DPO, Peacebuilding Fund) with follow-through mechanisms. It also notes that visa barriers and limited resources make local peacebuilders' presence in international forums harder.

**MFAN** — *Centering the Moral Imperative and Local Leadership in the Next Chapter of U.S. Humanitarian Aid* (2 September 2026, Meghan Armistead, Catholic Relief Services). The commentary recommends increasing direct funding to local organizations "as directly as possible" and ensuring fair indirect cost recovery so local actors can sustain operations.

**NEAR Network** — *Grand Bargain New Report: Survey Analysis* (14 September 2026, with A4EP). The analysis draws on a survey of more than 350 local and national civil society organizations in the Global South, timed to the ten-year anniversary of the World Humanitarian Summit, the end of Grand Bargain 3.0 and negotiations on a successor platform. The accessible page text did not include the quantitative findings; they should be read in the full report.

### Theme 2: Intermediaries and pooled funds as routes to local actors

**ALNAP** — SOHS 2026 in brief. Modelling cited in the report estimates that "local intermediaries could deliver programming 32% more cost-efficiently than international" counterparts, and the report notes that this advantage has not translated into increased resource allocation. Country-Based Pooled Funds directed "46% of funding to LNAs in 2025, rising to 55% including subgrants," better than the system average but still a minority share.

**MFAN** — 2 September commentary. Recommends that, where funding flows through international entities, the U.S. "require that implementers channel at least 25% of funding onward to local organizations." It calls for pooled fund governance that ensures "local actor engagement and decision making, including around funding priorities," and endorses existing mechanisms such as UN OCHA pooled funds and the START Network as models for new State Department funding.

**NEAR Network** — 14 September survey analysis. The page indicates the report addresses funding flows, intermediary roles and pooled funding models, including NEAR's Change Fund. Specific findings were not retrievable (see above).

### Peacebuilding measurement

**Peace Direct** — 2 September article (above). Communities define progress through relational change such as increased trust and reduced fear, and prevention success ("what never happened") is hard to capture in formal indicators.

## Editorial note

*This section is TST-side interpretation, not source findings.* The ALNAP and MFAN items point in the same direction on mechanisms: ALNAP's evidence that country-based pooled funds and local intermediaries outperform the system average on reaching local actors is the same evidence base MFAN's commentary draws on when it asks the State Department to adopt pooled fund governance with local decision-making and a 25% onward-flow requirement. This is relevant to TST's work on intermediaries and alternative pooled funds. No finding this month addresses the "complementary" criterion directly.
